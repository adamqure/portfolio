FAQ

Working with a connected-device software engineer

What kind of projects do you take on?

Software for products that talk to hardware: Bluetooth LE pairing and background sync, Wi-Fi device provisioning, offline-first data, over-the-air firmware updates, and the mobile apps and backends around them. I’ve done this for consumer devices, wearables, remote patient monitoring and a regulated medical device platform.

How do engagements work?

Fixed-scope milestones with a clear deliverable, from a one-week architecture review or Bluetooth audit to a full build. Most projects start with a free 30-minute intro call.

Do you have experience with regulated medical device software?

Yes. At Nevro I led compliance-critical mobile features for a regulated medical device platform, served as independent reviewer on firmware projects, and built a localization and audit pipeline that cut verification time by about 99.8%.

Which platforms and languages do you work in?

Swift, SwiftUI and UIKit on iOS; Kotlin Multiplatform, Android and Flutter; server-side Swift (Vapor) and Python on AWS; REST and GraphQL APIs. I work directly with firmware teams across the device and software boundary.

What makes your Bluetooth work different?

I co-invented a patented multi-tier Bluetooth security framework for mobile-to-device communication (U.S. Patent No. 12,652,273), and I test against simulated devices so pairing, reconnect and sync failures show up in CI, not in the field.

Where are you based?

The Atlanta metro area in Georgia, USA. I’m available for remote freelance work with teams anywhere, and I also work in Mandarin, including technical specs and communication with manufacturers.
